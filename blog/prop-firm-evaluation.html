How to Pass a Prop Firm Trading Evaluation

By Ali Raza · Axiom Trading Journal

Prop firm evaluations aren't really testing whether you can find good trades — most traders who fail a challenge already know how to trade. What actually gets accounts funded is risk control and consistency under a strict rulebook. Here's what to focus on if you want to pass on your first attempt instead of your third.

01 Read the Rulebook Before You Place a Single Trade

Every prop firm has its own daily loss limit, maximum drawdown, minimum trading days, and profit target — and these details vary a lot between firms. Traders fail challenges they were technically skilled enough to pass simply because they didn't know the exact daily loss limit until they'd already breached it. Read the rules twice before funding the challenge, not after.

02 Treat the Daily Loss Limit as a Hard Stop

Most evaluations fail on the daily loss limit, not the overall drawdown limit — because a bad day compounds fast when a trader keeps trying to "win it back" within the same session. Decide your personal daily stop (tighter than the firm's limit) before the day starts, and walk away from the charts once you hit it, no exceptions.

03 Undersize Relative to What You'd Normally Risk

The math of an evaluation rewards surviving long enough to hit the profit target, not maximizing the speed you get there. Risking 0.5-1% per trade instead of the 2% you might risk on a personal account gives you far more room for the losing trades that are guaranteed to happen along the way.

04 Respect Consistency Rules

Many firms require that no single trading day account for more than a set percentage of total profits (commonly 20-40%), specifically to filter out one lucky oversized trade. Spreading gains across multiple trading days — rather than swinging for one huge win — is often the difference between a payout and a rule violation, even if the account is net profitable.

05 Hit the Minimum Trading Days Early, Not Last-Minute

Evaluations with a minimum number of trading days are easy to forget about until the deadline is close, which then pushes traders into rushed, oversized trades just to qualify in time. Log a small, low-risk trade on quiet days early in the challenge specifically to bank the day requirement while there's no time pressure.

06 Stop Trading Once You Hit the Profit Target

Once the profit target is reached, every additional trade is pure downside risk with no upside toward passing — a losing streak after the target is hit is how traders undo weeks of good work. Passing isn't about proving you can keep winning; it's about proving you can stop.

07 Journal the Evaluation Like a Real Account

Evaluations often fail for behavioral reasons — a bigger-than-usual position after a losing day, an entry taken outside your normal setup out of frustration — and those patterns are invisible without a written record. Logging every trade during the challenge, including the reasoning behind it, makes it possible to catch a rule-breaking habit before it costs the account rather than after.
