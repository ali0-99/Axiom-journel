TRADING FUNDAMENTALS

Common Forex Trading Mistakes Beginners Make

By Ali Raza · Axiom Trading Journal

Most beginner Forex traders don't lose money because they lack a strategy — they lose money because of a handful of repeatable, avoidable mistakes that have nothing to do with market analysis. Here are the ones that show up most often, and what actually fixes each one.

01 Overtrading

Taking too many trades, too often, usually out of boredom or a need to "stay in the market," is one of the fastest ways to erode an account through spread and commission costs alone — before a single bad trade even happens. If you find yourself opening a chart just to find something, anything, to trade, that's a sign to step back. A simple fix: set a maximum number of trades per day before you start trading, not after.

02 Ignoring Risk-to-Reward Ratio

Many beginners focus entirely on being "right" — chasing a high win rate — while ignoring how much they stand to lose versus gain on each trade. A strategy that wins 70% of the time can still lose money overall if the losses are consistently bigger than the wins. Calculate risk-to-reward before entering every trade, not after it closes.

03 Moving the Stop-Loss

Widening a stop-loss mid-trade because "it'll probably come back" is one of the most common ways a small, planned loss turns into a large, unplanned one. The stop-loss is a decision made with a clear head before the trade — moving it under pressure, in the moment, defeats its entire purpose.

04 Revenge Trading

Jumping straight back into the market after a loss to "win it back" is an emotional reaction, not a strategic one, and it tends to compound one loss into several. If you notice you're sizing up a trade right after a loss specifically to recover money faster, that's revenge trading — the fix is a mandatory pause (even 30 minutes) before the next entry after any loss.

05 No Trading Journal

Without a record of entries, exits, reasoning, and outcomes, it's almost impossible to tell the difference between a strategy that genuinely doesn't work and a good strategy that was executed poorly. Traders who don't journal tend to repeat the same mistakes for months without ever noticing the pattern, simply because nothing is written down to compare against.

06 Risking Too Much Per Trade

Risking 5-10% of an account on a single trade means only a few consecutive losses — which happen to every strategy eventually — can wipe out a large portion of the account. Most experienced traders risk 1-2% per trade specifically so that a losing streak is uncomfortable, not catastrophic.
